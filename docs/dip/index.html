Dip Buy CSP Strategy

This page identifies the top 20 S&P 500 stocks by single-day percentage decline and surfaces cash-secured put opportunities on those names. The mean-reversion thesis: oversold stocks tend to bounce, and selling puts captures elevated IV while giving you a strike that's already discounted from a lower stock price.

Extra columns on this page

Column What it means
Day Chg Today's % price change. Deeper red = bigger drop. Darker shading at ≤−5%.
Vol Ratio Today's volume ÷ 30-day average volume. ≥2× signals unusually high selling pressure (potential capitulation); shown in blue/violet.
↑52W Lo How far the current price sits above the 52-week low. Low values (red) mean the stock is near its annual floor — more cautious territory; high values mean today's drop is a blip in an otherwise healthy trend.

What to watch for

  • Vol Spike ≥2× — heavy selling may indicate capitulation and a near-term floor.
  • High ↑52W Lo (≥20%) — stock still well above its annual low; today's drop is likely news-driven or sector rotation rather than a structural breakdown.
  • Earnings-cleared — if earnings just passed, IV is elevated from the run-up but drops aren't earnings risk — good premium, lower binary risk.
  • Avoid: near 52W low + unknown earnings — catching a falling knife with earnings uncertainty is the highest-risk combination.
  • IV is elevated after a drop — that means more premium but also faster IV crush if the stock stabilizes. Close early at 50% profit rather than holding to expiry.

Hard filters (same as main scanner)

  • Put options only, expiring within 21 calendar days
  • Absolute delta ≤ 20% (slightly wider than the base scanner to account for inflated IV)
  • Bid > $0, open interest ≥ 100
  • Bid/ask spread ≤ 20% of the mid price
  • Earnings before expiration excluded by default

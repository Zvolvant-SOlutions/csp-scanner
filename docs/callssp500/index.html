How the score works — Calls to Sell

This tab finds out-of-the-money calls to sell (covered calls or, if you prefer, cash/margin-secured) that pay a good premium while keeping a low probability of assignment. Every contract is above today’s stock price, has an absolute delta of 15% or less, expires in 30-90 days, and collects at least $100 of premium. The list is capped at the global top 100 by score.

Reading the columns for a short call: Target = the strike you’d be assigned at (sell your shares at this price); Cushion = how far the stock must rally before the strike is reached (upside room); Breakeven = strike + premium; PoP% = probability the call expires worthless (you keep the premium and your shares); Cash Req = the cost of 100 shares to cover it. A higher delta or smaller cushion means a higher chance the stock blows through your strike and your shares get called away.

Hard filters (excluded before scoring)

  • Call options only, strike above the current price (out of the money)
  • 30-90 calendar days to expiration
  • Absolute delta ≤ 15%
  • Premium (bid × 100) ≥ $100 per contract
  • Bid > $0, open interest ≥ 100
  • Bid/ask spread ≤ 20% of the mid price

Score components (max 100 pts)

Component Max pts What earns the points
Premium collected 25 Actual dollar premium per contract (bid × 100). ~$500+ earns full points — this is the “gather a good amount of premium” driver.
Upside cushion 25 How far the stock must rally to reach the strike. (strike − price) ÷ price. 15% room = full points.
Delta safety 20 Lower delta = lower chance of assignment. 0% delta → full 20 pts; the 15% cap → 0 pts.
Liquidity 15 Open interest (log-scaled, up to 10 pts) plus tight bid/ask spread (up to 5 pts).
Earnings risk 15 No earnings within 30 days → 15 pts. Earnings before expiry → 0 pts (not filtered out; the premium partly pays for the risk).

These are low-probability-of-assignment trades by design — most expire worthless and you keep the premium — but a sharp rally can still put the stock above your strike. Covered calls cap your upside at the strike; naked calls carry unlimited risk. Not investment advice — verify in your broker before trading.
